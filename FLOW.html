The one-sentence version

The cryptography is competently implemented and completely undermined by key management: every secret the app relies on — the XXTEA script key, the AES-256-GCM key, the HMAC key, the WebSocket salt — is a static string that ships inside the APK. On top of that, three whole transports have no integrity protection at all, and the withdrawal path puts the payment PIN and SMS OTP in a GET URL query string.

Is the transport even encrypted?

payapi is assigned at login from the server response (this.payapi = t.payapi), so the scheme is server-decided. The manifest sets usesCleartextTraffic="true" and res/8G.xml sets a base-config cleartext policy for all domains — so http:// is permitted. If payapi is ever served over http, the PIN and OTP above are on the wire in cleartext as well as in the logs.

XXTEA recovery

jsb_set_xxtea_key   exported @ 0x6e74ec
  └─ PLT stub       0x313100   (GOT 0x1530ec8, rela.plt #1974)
      └─ only caller  0x3330c0
           0x3330a8  adrp x1, #0x112c000
           0x3330b0  add  x1, x1, #0xb05      -> .rodata 0x112cb05
           0x3330c0  bl   #0x313100
.rodata @ 0x112cb05:  Cocos Game\0 b32a2160-0c63-41\0 jsb-adapter/jsb-builtin.js\0

Three deviations from textbook XXTEA, transcribed from the core at 0x70b834: y is loaded once before the round loop and carried across rounds; the p==0 step uses z = v[n-1], not v[0]; and the length word must satisfy n·4-7 ≤ len ≤ n·4-4. Getting these right produced a byte-exact re-encryption of all 9 .jsc files.

The key is a truncated UUID — 14 hex characters at fixed positions plus two literal dashes, so ~56 bits of entropy. Irrelevant in practice: it is a plaintext string in .rodata. The format is the real problem — XXTEA has no authentication, and the plaintext is gzip, whose first 10 bytes are a fixed header, handing an attacker a free known-plaintext block at a known offset.

What correct looks like

  1. Never ship a long-lived symmetric key — ECDH (X25519) at login, HKDF to a per-session key.
  2. Make the envelope mandatory, or drop the bespoke scheme for TLS with certificate pinning.
  3. Canonicalise before signing, and actually call validateTimestamp.
  4. Add a nonce plus a server-side replay cache.
  5. Sign requests too, with an idempotency key on every wallet mutation.
  6. Constant-time compare everywhere (i === o today is timing-observable).
  7. Remove the ungated response-body logging.
  8. Replace the delta cipher with EncryptedSharedPreferences / Android Keystore.
  9. Sign the hot-update manifest asymmetrically, over HTTPS.
